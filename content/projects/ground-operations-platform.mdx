An aircraft turnaround is a coordination problem with a clock on it. Between
arrival and departure, a fixed set of ground handling tasks — baggage, fuel,
catering, cleaning, pushback — has to happen in a workable order, carried out by
drivers who each have a location, a vehicle, a skill set and a shift.

Doing that by radio works until it doesn't. One delayed inbound reshuffles
everything downstream, and the cost of a bad assignment compounds across the
rest of the shift.

## What I built

The assignment engine matches tasks to drivers under real constraints — time
windows, equipment, location, and who is actually free. It runs continuously
rather than as a nightly plan, so when the ramp changes the schedule changes
with it.

Around that sits the operational software the dispatchers and ramp crews use:
the thing that makes the optimisation visible and overridable, because an
engine that can't be overruled by someone standing on the apron is an engine
that gets switched off.
